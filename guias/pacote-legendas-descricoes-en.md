### Description
```
Broccoli is the one vegetable here that does NOT make its own brine.
Cabbage does. Beetroot does. Broccoli barely releases anything - so you have to
add water, and salt alone will not do it.

THE BRINE - 2.5%, not 2%
1 liter of water + 25 g of non-iodized salt. Broccoli is a brassica, like
cauliflower, and brassicas need a little more salt. Stir until it disappears.

THE CUT - 1-inch pieces, never shredded
Cut small and it turns to mush in two days. And mushy broccoli never comes back.
Keep the stems - they are the part that stays crunchy.

THE ORANGE - zest yes, juice only a little
The peel adds aroma and does not stop the ferment. The juice is sugar, and sugar

stops being a ferment.

out a perfectly good jar. The smell fades. The crunch does not.

WHITE FILM vs FUZZ
A thin white film on top is kahm yeast. Skim it off and keep going.
Fuzzy green, black or pink - throw the jar out.

NEVER START FROM FROZEN
Freezing breaks the cells. Frozen broccoli turns to mush before the ferment
even starts.

Ready in 5 to 10 days. Start tasting on day 3. When you like it, it goes in the
fridge - that is what stops the clock. Keeps 4 to 6 months.

### Description
```
A 1.5-liter soda bottle, 200 ml of warm water, 50 g of sugar and 1 g of yeast.
That is the whole trap. Five minutes, and it costs you nothing.

WHY IT WORKS
Mosquitoes do not hunt you by sight. They follow carbon dioxide - the gas you
breathe out. Yeast eating sugar makes exactly the same gas. The bottle is not
the trick. The gas is. The bottle is only the cage.

STEP 1 - CUT THE TOP OFF
Cut about a third of the way down, right at the shoulder. A 1.5 L bottle is the
classic size. Keep both halves - nothing is thrown away, the top becomes the
funnel.

STEP 2 - 200 ml WARM WATER + 50 g SUGAR
Warm water from the tap. Mix until the sugar is gone. 50 g is about three
heaped tablespoons.

STEP 3 - COOL IT, THEN 1 g YEAST
Hot water kills the yeast, so wait until it is lukewarm. 1 g is about a quarter
of a teaspoon. Do not stir - it dissolves on its own, and stirring changes
nothing.

STEP 4 - FLIP THE TOP OVER
Push it in neck-first. The neck becomes a funnel: they fly in and cannot find
the way back out. Press it down firmly so the rim sits just under the edge of
the bottle.

STEP 5 - WRAP IT IN PAPER
A sheet of newspaper or a plastic bag. They look for dark places. Leave the top
open - dark, but never sealed. Seal it and nothing can get in.

STEP 6 - DARK CORNER, THEN WAIT
Low, in the shade, out of the rain, near where they breed. Bubbles mean it is
working. Replace it every 2 weeks: when the bubbles stop, the trap is dead.

THE PART NOBODY TELLS YOU
This trap catches a few. Standing water breeds hundreds. Dump the buckets, the
plant saucers, the old tires and the clogged gutters first - that is the real
fix. The bottle helps. It is not magic.

# 17. CUCUMBER + APPLE — SUGAR IS NOT TANNIN

Cucumber already has its own video (#6, the 4-day one). This one does **not**
repeat it: it answers the question that video leaves open.

**Everyone drops apple into the pickle jar "to keep it crunchy". Apple is not a
tannin source.** The tannin sources that actually work are grape, oak, bay,
horseradish and cherry leaves, plus a pinch of black tea. The apple is there for
**sugar** — it makes the jar go faster and taste fruitier — and the bay leaf is
still the thing holding the crunch. Apple is a *sugar*, not a *tannin*.

> Post it **after #6 (cucumber)**. Without that one the tannin rule lands with no
> context and this video looks like it contradicts itself.

### Title
```
Apple does not keep pickles crunchy - the leaf does

**Alternatives:**

### Description
```
Everyone says to drop apple into the pickle jar to keep it crunchy. That is not how it works.

The apple brings SUGAR. Sugar feeds the bacteria, so the jar moves faster and tastes
fruitier. That is a real job - it is just not the job people think it is.

Crunch comes from TANNIN, and tannin comes from a leaf: bay, grape, oak, horseradish,
cherry - or a pinch of black tea. No tannin, no crunch. The pectinase enzyme eats the
pectin and the cucumber turns to mush.

Cucumber to fill the jar + 1 apple, in wedges.
3% brine = 1 litre of water + 30 g of salt.
Bay leaf in. Everything under the brine.
4 to 6 days - the apple makes it go faster, so start tasting on day 3.

months.

# 21. SOURDOUGH STARTER — FLOUR AND WATER

### Title
```
Sourdough starter: two microbes in one jar
```

**Alternatives:**
- `Flour and water is all it takes - here is what is happening inside`
- `Why your starter smells like vinegar (and how to change that)`
- `Warm or cold? That is what decides how sour your bread is`

### Description
```
A starter is not a recipe. It is a culture - and there are two organisms living in it.

Wild YEAST is what lifts the bread: it eats the sugars and gives off carbon dioxide, and
the gluten net traps that gas. LACTIC ACID BACTERIA are what make it sour, and they
outnumber the yeast by roughly 100 to one. The bacteria make two different acids:
lactic acid is mild and milky, acetic acid is sharp like vinegar. The ratio between the

- 30-35 °C favours the bacteria -> milder, more lactic
- 20-24 °C favours the acetic side -> sharper, more sour

flour and a cup of water do not weigh the same, and the culture notices.

is holding enough gas and it is ready.

Two things that are not the same thing:

- Dark liquid on top is ALCOHOL. That is a hungry starter, not a dead one. Stir it back
  in and feed it.
- Pink or orange patches are MOULD. There is no way back from that one. Throw the whole
  jar out and start again.

Bread is the slowest thing on this channel and the only one you keep alive. Everything
else you make once and eat.

### Description
```
This is the SECOND fermentation. The kombucha is already made - now it goes into a sealed
bottle with fruit, and everything about it changes.

  not kill it. A bottle that is firm and still warm is a bottle that keeps building.

The test is your hand, not a timer: firm means ready.

# 28. BEET KVASS WITH GARLIC — THE WEAKEST BRINE HERE

### Title
```
Beet kvass with garlic: the weakest brine in the book
```

**Alternatives:**
- `One teaspoon of salt per litre - and why that changes everything`
- `Why beet kvass is salted by the water, not by the beet`
- `The brine that is a third of sauerkraut's`

### Description
```
Beet kvass is beet, water, salt and a clove of garlic - and it runs on the weakest brine
in this whole series.

ONE TEASPOON PER LITRE

That is about 6 g of salt in 1 litre of water: roughly 0.6% salt. Compare it to the rest of
the jars:

- sauerkraut runs at 2% - about three times more;
- a pickle brine runs at 3.5 to 5% - about eight times more.

And look at what the percentage is measured ON. In sauerkraut the salt is a percentage of
the WEIGHT OF THE CABBAGE, and the cabbage makes its own liquid. In kvass you add the water,
so the salt is a percentage of THE WATER. "2%" means nothing until you say 2% of what.

WHY THE WEAK BRINE CHANGES EVERYTHING

A weak brine cannot defend its own surface, and that is the whole reason this jar needs a
lid more than any other in the series: keep every piece under the liquid, seal it, and burp
it once a day.

Two ways to lose the batch:

- SHRED the beet and it gives up too much sugar too fast - you get alcoholic beet beer. Dice
  it into 2 cm cubes instead, and do not peel it: the bacteria you want are on the skin.
- Let it turn VISCOUS or SLIMY and it is gone. That is not a stage, it is a failure: throw
  it out and start again.

THEN

4 to 7 days at 18-21 C. Start tasting from day three - you are waiting for a clean lactic
tartness, not for a number of days. Strain it, chill it, and refill the same jar with fresh
brine for a second batch: it ferments faster and comes out lighter in colour and flavour.

Beet is the fuel here: about 7 g of sugar per 100 g, more than any other root in this
series. Kvass stays under 1% alcohol by volume, but it is not zero, and it keeps rising
while the jar sits. Drink it fresh and keep it cold.
```
